## How to use these case studies

Use these as product reasoning references, not visual themes to copy. The Sanvithi cases are strong for expert workflows, workspace consolidation, high-density UI, AI trust, and consent. The Amy La cases are strong for creative iteration, lightweight mobile flows, social confidence, and research-led participation design.

Inspect visuals that show:

- The primary work surface before and after redesign.
- Dense tools being consolidated into a single workspace.
- AI recommendations with explanation, control, or consent.
- Mobile flows that make private creation or social joining feel low-risk.
- Research artifacts that change the product direction.

Skip decorative portfolio covers unless the task is portfolio storytelling.

## Case 1: Explora

Source: https://sanvithi.com/explora

### Core lesson

Expert tools should reduce context switching before they add more features. Explora consolidates fragmented biotech workflows into an IDE-like environment so scientists can compare models, run simulations, and inspect results without jumping across legacy tools and spreadsheets.

### Design moves to reuse

- Treat the product as a workbench, not a collection of pages.
- Preserve the user's scientific or analytical context while switching between tools.
- Replace export-heavy workflows with direct comparison and visualization inside the product.
- Design for asynchronous processing: show ongoing tasks, let users continue working, and make processing state visible.
- Build a lean design system around repeated expert patterns rather than generic components.

### Visuals worth inspecting

- Hero workspace showing model viewer, plotter, properties, and path-finding surfaces.
- Plotter view that overlays multiple model results.
- Ongoing-tasks/status treatment for asynchronous simulations.
- Any before/process diagrams showing legacy tool fragmentation.
- Conceptual AI assistant or conversational homepage, if the task involves AI expert support.

### Red flags to catch in future designs

- Expert users must copy, export, or re-render data in external tools to understand results.
- A workflow pauses while processing instead of allowing parallel work.
- Navigation consumes space that should belong to the primary canvas.
- A design system grows broad before it supports the most repeated expert tasks.

## Case 2: Mira.ai

Source: https://sanvithi.com/miraai

### Core lesson

AI in sensitive health-adjacent products must be explainable, consentful, and grounded in the user's body/context. Mira.ai pairs a wearable and companion app, but the product value comes from plain-language reasoning, user approval, and careful boundaries around physiological intervention.

### Design moves to reuse

- Explain recommendations in plain language at the moment they appear.
- Make the system's input sources visible: symptoms, nutrition, prescriptions, wearable signals, history, and restrictions.
- Keep intervention controls simple, visible, and close to the moment of action.
- Treat privacy and local/on-device boundaries as part of the product UX, not backend trivia.
- Use AI to adapt and support, not to obscure responsibility.

### Visuals worth inspecting

- Apple Watch plus mobile companion screens.
- Nutrition recommendation screens that explain why a meal or supplement is suggested.
- Pulse/intervention state with a centered stop control.
- Bio-digital-twin or system-layer diagram.
- Prescription upload/context-aware AI concept.

### Red flags to catch in future designs

- AI recommendations appear without a reason the user can understand.
- Sensitive actions happen automatically without explicit consent or an obvious stop path.
- The interface uses magical language where user trust needs evidence.
- The product asks for intimate data without showing why it is needed and where it goes.

## Case 3: Manage

Source: https://sanvithi.com/manage

### Core lesson

Operational enterprise tools should protect the workspace and reduce repetitive physical work. Manage's redesign centers the floor plan, collapses clutter, exposes critical fixture status, and replaces mouse-heavy right-click workflows with faster direct manipulation.

### Design moves to reuse

- Make the real-world object or canvas the center of the interface.
- Collapse navigation and inspection surfaces when they compete with the workspace.
- Surface status persistently when users must monitor many objects.
- Replace repeated right-click or menu-chain actions with batch actions, direct manipulation, drag-and-drop, or contextual panels.
- Design for field conditions: small trackpads, time pressure, visual scanning, and error avoidance.

### Visuals worth inspecting

- Floor-plan workspace with sensors/fixtures.
- Collapsible navigation and right-side detail/status panel.
- Before/after treatment of high-density data.
- Grouping or assignment interactions that replace repeated context menus.
- AI-assisted predictive grouping concept if the task involves automation for operations.

### Red flags to catch in future designs

- Critical state is visible only on hover.
- Users must repeat the same mouse-heavy interaction hundreds of times.
- Panels crowd the map, canvas, timeline, or other core work surface.
- Visual polish hides the fact that the interaction model is still slow.

## Case 4: Untitled x Lyrics

Source: https://amylalai.com/untitledlyrics-fromhome

### Core lesson

Creative AI should support the artist's existing practice instead of replacing authorship. The lyric feature starts from how musicians actually create: loop a section, improvise, record rough takes, then generate and tune lyric options from the artist's own material.

### Design moves to reuse

- Start from the user's natural creative ritual before adding AI.
- Make experimentation private, reversible, and low-pressure.
- Let AI respond to the user's own input rather than creating from a blank prompt.
- Visualize uncertain product decisions as interface flows to reveal friction.
- Provide fine-tuning controls that preserve authorship and taste.

### Visuals worth inspecting

- Loop creation and recording screens.
- Lyrics generation screen tied to the user's recorded take.
- Rhyme-style/fine-tuning controls.
- Flow showing "user creates loop -> records experiments -> generates lyrics -> edits."
- Research or learning sections about artist resistance to prompt-based AI.

### Red flags to catch in future designs

- AI takes the creative lead before the user has contributed meaningful source material.
- The user cannot compare, edit, or reject generated options.
- The interface makes rough drafts feel too public or final.
- The product copies a generic AI prompt flow instead of fitting the domain's practice.

## Case 5: Play

Source: https://amylalai.com/play-case-study

### Core lesson

Social coordination products must reduce uncertainty before asking users to join. Play focuses on remote workers and hobby meetups, making events discoverable by interest, location, skill level, social context, and clear join/request flows.

### Design moves to reuse

- Show the social and logistical context before the call to action.
- Make joining feel reversible or request-based when social risk is high.
- Use research to choose the wedge: coordination difficulty, shared interests, remote-worker loneliness, and activity discovery.
- Support both discovery and confidence: map, recommendations, event details, host/group cues, and messages.
- Provide enough identity and safety signals without turning the flow into heavy onboarding.

### Visuals worth inspecting

- Event detail screen with location, time, players, skill level, and creator.
- Map/search discovery screen.
- Profile/upcoming events/activity history screen.
- User interview artifacts and ideation options.
- Final mobile navigation showing home, messages, gallery, and profile.

### Red flags to catch in future designs

- The user must commit before knowing who, where, when, skill level, or group norms.
- Discovery is only a feed, with no spatial or interest context.
- The product solves event creation but not social confidence.
- The UI treats joining strangers like a simple ecommerce conversion.

## Combined heuristics

### Expert tools

Design for continuity of attention. Minimize tool-hopping, preserve the work canvas, support batch operations, expose status, and treat asynchronous processing as a first-class workflow.

### AI products

Design for earned trust. Show why a recommendation exists, what data it used, what the user can change, and where the boundary of automation sits.

### Creative products

Design for authorship. Let users generate from their own material, iterate safely, compare alternatives, and keep final control.

### Social products

Design for confidence. Before asking users to join, show enough logistics, identity, norms, and reversibility to reduce anxiety.
